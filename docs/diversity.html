How varied the cropping is

Four measures of crop diversity for each district.

A district can grow twenty crops and still put four fifths of its land under one of them. A plain count of crops would call that district varied. The four measures here separate the number of crops from how land is shared among them.

What the four measures do

The count of crops grown treats every crop the same. A crop on one hectare counts as much as the staple. The effective number of crops is the number of equally sized crops that would give the observed diversity, so it gives more weight to the crops that cover most of the land. The dominant-weighted count goes further and depends almost entirely on the largest crops.

Evenness is the effective number divided by the plain count. It removes the count and measures only how evenly land is spread. A district can grow many crops and still score low.

Reading the map

When you switch from the count to the effective number, the eastern rice belt and the northwestern wheat belt get lighter. These districts grow an ordinary number of crops, but the leading crop takes much of the land.

Karnataka, interior Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan keep their colour on all four measures. Their cropping is varied by every measure.
