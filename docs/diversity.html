Crop diversity in each district, on four measures

Each district’s average over the years it reports, 1997–98 to 2019–20.

This map shades every Indian district by how varied its cropping is. You can switch between four measures of that variety. They differ in how much weight each gives to the small crops a district grows on a little of its land.

How to read this map

Brighter cyan means more varied cropping on the measure you have picked, and dark slate means less. The buttons above the map switch the measure. Hover over a district to see its count of crops, its effective number of crops, its evenness and its largest crops with their share of its land. The count of crops is how many different crops a district grows at all. The effective number of crops is the number of equally sized crops that would give the same spread of land, so a district split evenly across 20 crops scores 20.

What the four measures do

The count of crops treats every crop the same, so a crop on one hectare counts as much as the staple. The effective number of crops weighs each crop by the land it covers. The number of main crops leans harder on the largest crops and depends almost entirely on the one or two that take most of the land.

Evenness is the effective number of crops divided by the count of crops. It runs from close to zero up to one and describes only how evenly land is spread across the crops a district grows. A district that grows many crops can still have low evenness.

Where the measures disagree

Chhattisgarh and Assam grow a lot of crops and are bright on the count of crops. Switch to the effective number of crops and they turn dark, because rice takes a large share of their land.

Karnataka, interior Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan stay among the brighter districts on the count of crops, the effective number of crops and the number of main crops. On evenness they sit near the middle.
