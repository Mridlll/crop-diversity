Food energy against variety

Whether growing a wider range of crops costs a district food energy.

A common worry is that growing more kinds of crops lowers output. If that were true, the diverse districts would produce less food energy from the same land. The data do not show this.

Interactive

Food energy per hectare, and the four groups

Choose a view. Hover a district for its crops and its energy output.

Energy is the food energy in a district's own harvest, per hectare of its cropped area, averaged across its years. Coconut is counted as edible meat rather than as whole nuts, which is how it is reported.

What is being measured

Each crop's harvest is converted to food energy using standard energy values per hundred grams. These are summed across the district's crops and divided by its cropped area. The result is the food energy one hectare in that district produces in a year. It measures what the land yields, not what people eat or who gets it.

The median district produces about 5.9 million kilocalories a hectare. Values span two orders of magnitude, so the map uses a logarithmic scale. A linear scale would show almost nothing.

The four groups

Splitting districts at the median on both measures gives four groups of roughly equal size. If variety cost energy, the diverse and energy-rich group would be nearly empty. It holds about as many districts as the others.

The concentrated and energy-rich group is the irrigated cereal belt, which gets a lot of energy from a narrow rotation. The diverse and energy-poor group is mostly rainfed and upland. The two groups face different problems.
