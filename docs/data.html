Data and methods

Where the numbers come from and how districts were matched

The measures of crop diversity come from crop area for 54 crops over 23 years. Village records summed to each district describe its land, irrigation, markets and caste make-up.

01 · Sources

The 93 district measures are sums of village records, set beside crop statistics compiled for each district.

Width is the number of variables. Ratios built from a source are counted with it. Night lights and the Economic Census are district files, and the other SHRUG sources are village records summed to the district. The six development measures outside the 93 are night lights, non-farm businesses and the four shares of villages that make up the connectivity average. Hover or tap a source to list its variables. SHRUG 2.1, Development Data Lab; ISB India Data Portal.

Crop area, production and yield for 54 crops come from district statistics that the ISB India Data Portal compiles from Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare publications. They run from 1997–98 to 2019–20 and cover every reported season, from kharif and rabi to whole-year crops. The crops fall into ten categories: cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fibre crops, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugar, fodder, and drugs and narcotics. Only reported crop area is included, so land outside the reporting system, such as kitchen gardens, is missing.

SHRUG 2.1 is an open dataset that gives Indian administrative records a single village identifier, so sources collected years apart can be joined village by village. Each village record is summed to the district that contains it, and a district value is a total or a share across its villages. SHRUG holds no crop areas, which is why crop diversity has to be measured from the district statistics.

Most district measures come from Mission Antyodaya, a 2019–20 rural survey in which each gram panchayat reports what its villages have, from irrigated land to a mandi or a food storage warehouse. The answers come from the panchayat's own report, so levels can run high. A share of villages is the fraction of a district's villages that report a facility. The average district has a mandi in 3.0 percent of its villages, a food storage warehouse in 8.4 percent, a weekly haat in 14.3 percent and a producer organisation in 23.1 percent. Every village counts the same, whatever its size.

The 2011 Population Census adds land use, irrigation by source, caste shares and the farm workforce. Land ownership comes from the 2012 Socio-Economic and Caste Census (SECC). Night lights seen from satellite in 2023 and non-farm businesses counted in the 2013 Economic Census give two development measures that the facility comparisons hold constant.

02 · Timing

Each district measure comes from a single year between 2011 and 2023, set against crop records that run over 23 years.

Crop years are labelled by their starting year. A district enters the crop record in the year it first reports. 427 of the 606 analysed districts have all 23 years, and the median is 23. Hover or tap the line for the count in each year.

The number of districts reporting crops grows from 484 in 1997–98 to 699 in 2019–20, because new districts were being created throughout the period. Of the 606 districts analysed, 427 have all 23 years and 67 have fewer than 20.

Each district's crop diversity is an average over the years it reports. The district measures each come from one year. The census measures are from 2011 and 2012, the Economic Census from 2013, the facility measures from 2019–20 and night lights from 2023, a few years after the last crop year. Every comparison therefore sets a long-run cropping average against a single year of village records. The comparisons are therefore between districts at one time, with no measure of change within a district.

03 · Matching districts

722 of the 725 districts in the crop record match a 2011 census district.

One square per district. Unmatched: Mumbai, Daman and Diu. Matching uses the 2011 census frame that SHRUG is built on, with 631 districts. Hover or tap a square for the district and the 2011 district it maps to.

SHRUG uses 2011 census district boundaries and the crop record uses present-day district names. Many districts were renamed or split after 2011, and Telangana became a state in 2014. The crop record lists 33 Telangana districts, and all of them trace back to ten districts that the 2011 census filed under Andhra Pradesh. Adilabad and Mahabubnagar have enough years under the Telangana name to enter the 606. Seven more of the ten, among them Karimnagar and Warangal, enter through the years up to 2012–13 that the crop record files under Andhra Pradesh.

An exact name match settles 534 districts. A list of renamed and newly created districts settles 141 more. A renamed district such as Kalaburagi, called Gulbarga in 2011, keeps its old record. Districts created after 2011 are traced to the district they were carved from. A similar name within the same state settles another 47. Twenty of those were too far apart to accept on the name alone, and each was read against the census district list. Mumbai, Daman and Diu stay unmatched, and none of them has meaningful farmland.

A district carved out after 2011 takes on its parent's village records, which describe the whole 2011 district. 64 of the 606 analysed districts share their 2011 district with at least one other district in the crop record. Inside the 606, only Amethi and Sultanpur share one with each other, so the 606 districts form 605 groups when uncertainty is measured.

04 · The sample

606 of the 725 districts have enough village records and enough years of crops to enter the results.

Coverage is villages in the survey over 2011 census villages in the same district. The ten-year rule removes 91 districts, most of them created after 2011. Hover or tap a removed slice to list its districts.

Nine matched districts have no Mission Antyodaya record at all. They are cities and small union territories: Puducherry's four districts, Chandigarh, Chennai, Hyderabad, Mumbai Suburban, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli. In another 16, the survey reaches fewer than a quarter of villages. Tripura accounts for eight of these, and its four 2011 districts sit between 5 and 16 percent coverage. Jammu and Kashmir has three and Manipur two. These districts are left out, because figures built from a small fraction of a district's villages cannot be compared with figures built from all of them.

The last rule asks for at least ten years of crop data, and 91 districts fall below it. Most of them were created after 2011. Thirty are in Telangana, where every district apart from Adilabad and Mahabubnagar has seven years or fewer under the Telangana name. Every result rests on the 606 districts that remain, across 30 states.

05 · Measuring diversity

The average district grows 21.2 crops in a year and has an effective number of crops of 4.9.

Effective number of crops = exp(−Σ p ln p), where p is a crop's share of the district's cropped area in a year. The number of main crops is 1 / Σ p². Evenness = effective number / count. Bins are half a crop wide. 606 districts.

A district's cropping pattern is a set of crops and the share of cropped land each one takes. A district with 70 percent of its land under cereals has a cereal share of 0.70, and the shares across crops add up to one.

The count of crops is how many crops have any land in a year. The effective number of crops weighs each crop by its land. It equals the number of equally sized crops that would spread land the same way. The average district grows 21.2 crops. Most of its land sits under two or three of them, and its effective number is 4.9. The number of main crops gives rare crops even less weight, and the average district has 3.5 of them. Evenness divides the effective number by the count and averages 0.254.

All three numbers are measured in crops, so a district with an effective number of 6 is twice as diverse as one with 3. The effective number of crops is the main outcome throughout. The three can only fall in the order count, effective number, number of main crops, because each step gives rare crops less weight.

06 · One year at a time

Counting crops within each year halves the link between a district's count and the number of years it reports.

725 districts in the crop record, spread a little sideways so they do not sit on top of each other. Pooled counts every crop that appeared in any year. Year-by-year counts crops within each year and averages across years. The line is the median count of crops at each number of years.

A district's count of crops can be built two ways. Pooling the land over all its years counts every crop that ever appears, so a district observed for 23 years collects crops it grew once, and it looks richer than an identical district observed for eight. Across the 725 districts, the pooled count averages 30.3 and correlates at 0.51 with the number of years observed.

Here each measure is computed within a year and then averaged over the years a district has. That brings the average count to 20.4 and the correlation with years observed to 0.26. Summing crop areas across seasons gives India 194.9 million hectares of cropped area in 2019–20. The Ministry of Agriculture puts gross sown area for that year at 211.4 million hectares, and the reported crops cover 92 percent of it, so adding the seasons together does not count a field twice.

07 · Checks against other sources

The village measures rank districts in close agreement with other sources.

correlation of levels, r rank correlation, rho

r compares levels and rho compares rankings, and 1 is perfect agreement. Cropping intensity is gross cropped area over net sown area, so 1.8 means the average field carries 1.8 crops a year. SECC is the Socio-Economic and Caste Census. Hover or tap a row for its means and district count.

Irrigated share from the 2019–20 village survey and the same share from the 2011 census village records rank 582 districts at a rank correlation of 0.812. Both are counts of the same land eight years apart, collected by different agencies. The survey's average is higher, 0.55 against 0.47, which fits panchayats reporting on their own villages. Scheduled Caste shares from the 2011 census and the 2012 SECC correlate at 0.942 across 609 districts, and Scheduled Tribe shares at 0.990.

Summing village records to the district reproduces SHRUG's own district file. Irrigated share, cropping intensity and mean holding size each correlate with the published district figures at 0.99 or more. Cropping intensity averages 1.75 across the 606 districts.

08 · What is estimated

Every comparison sets a district only against other districts in the same state.

Uncertainty ranges allow some districts to be more scattered than others and group districts from the same 2011 district. The 606 form 605 groups. Night lights 2023, Economic Census 2013. Hover or tap a row or a column heading for detail.

Each result is a regression of a crop diversity measure on one district measure. Where the relationship rises and then falls, the square of that measure is added too. Every regression gives each state its own baseline, the state baseline (economists call it a state fixed effect), so a district in Punjab is set only against other districts in Punjab. Anything that varies only between states, such as state agricultural policy, is absorbed by the baseline and cannot be estimated.

State baselines on their own account for 49 percent of the variation in the effective number of crops between districts. Adding irrigated share and its square raises that to 53 percent. Without state baselines the same irrigation terms account for 6.5 percent, and the peak moves from 28 percent of sown area irrigated to 51 percent.

Irrigation, mandis, food storage warehouses and fertiliser shops were put where terrain, groundwater and existing farming already favoured them. The results compare districts at one point in time, so they cannot show what adding a mandi to a district would do. Average farm size, cultivable area divided by the number of farmers, stands in for a farm census measure. It shows no link with diversity, +0.019 on the effective number of crops once irrigation and the state baseline are held constant, which may reflect a weak proxy. District procurement volumes are not published by any open source and are missing here.

How this was estimated

Crop data. Areas are summed across seasons for each crop, district and year. Whole-year rows overlap a named season in 441 crop-district-years, 0.6 percent of whole-year rows. The 2020–21 year is left out because only 13 districts report it. Summed cropped area is 182.2 million hectares in 1997–98 and 194.9 million hectares in 2019–20. Each index is computed within a year, and a district's value is its average across the years it has. Of the 725 districts in the crop record, 172 have fewer than 20 years.

Census shares count the whole population and SECC shares count rural households, which is why their levels differ. Against SHRUG's district file only ratios are compared, because the two cover slightly different sets of villages.

The comparisons. The irrigation curve regresses the effective number of crops on irrigated share and its square with the state baseline. The irrigation source comparison adds the canal and surface-water shares of villages, with groundwater left out as the comparison, and holds irrigated share and its square, log mean holding size and the Scheduled Tribe share constant. The single-measure results are shown first on their own and then holding irrigated share, its square and log mean holding size constant within the state baseline. The facility comparisons hold constant irrigated share and its square, log mean holding size, the Scheduled Tribe share, log night lights, non-farm establishments per 1,000 people and the connectivity average, with the state baseline, and apply a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the ten crop categories. Standard errors allow for unequal scatter and group districts by their 2011 district.
