Data and methods

Where the numbers come from and how districts were matched

Crop diversity comes from district crop records. What it is set against comes from village records added up to the district, most of them reported by the panchayats themselves.

01 · Sources

Most of what is known about a district's villages comes from what its panchayats report about themselves.

Width is the number of variables, with ratios counted under the source they are built from. Hover or tap a source to list its variables. SHRUG 2.1, Development Data Lab; ISB India Data Portal.

Crop area, production and yield for 54 crops come from district statistics that the ISB India Data Portal compiles from Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare publications. They cover every reported season, from kharif and rabi to whole-year crops. Land outside the reporting system, such as kitchen gardens, is missing.

SHRUG 2.1 is an open dataset that gives Indian administrative records a single village identifier, so sources collected years apart can be joined village by village. Each village record is summed to the district that contains it, and a district value is a total or a share across its villages. SHRUG holds no crop areas, which is why crop diversity has to be measured from the district statistics.

Most district measures come from Mission Antyodaya, a 2019–20 rural survey in which each gram panchayat reports what its villages have, from irrigated land to a mandi or a food storage warehouse. The answers come from the panchayat's own report, so levels can run high. A share of villages is the fraction of a district's villages that report a facility. The average district has a mandi in 3.0 percent of its villages, a food storage warehouse in 8.4 percent, a weekly haat in 14.3 percent and a producer organisation in 23.1 percent. Every village counts the same, whatever its size.

The 2011 Population Census adds land use, irrigation by source, caste shares and the farm workforce. Land ownership comes from the 2012 Socio-Economic and Caste Census (SECC). Night lights seen from satellite in 2023 and non-farm businesses counted in the 2013 Economic Census give two development measures that the facility comparisons hold constant.

02 · Timing

Village facilities are seen in a single year, so every comparison is between districts and none follows a district through time.

Crop years are labelled by their starting year. A district enters the crop record in the year it first reports. Hover or tap the line for the count in each year.

Because new districts were created throughout the period, the number reporting crops grows from 543 in 1998–99 to 699 in 2019–20.

A district's crop diversity is an average over the years it reports, while each village measure comes from one year. The census measures are from 2011 and 2012, the Economic Census from 2013, the facility measures from 2019–20 and night lights from 2023, a few years after the last crop year. Since a long-run cropping average is set against a single year of village records, the comparisons say how districts differ and cannot say how a district changed.

03 · Matching districts

A district carved out after 2011 carries the village records of the district it came from.

One square per district. Hover or tap a square for the district and the 2011 district it maps to.

SHRUG uses 2011 census district boundaries, whereas the crop record uses present-day district names. Many districts were renamed or split after 2011, and Telangana became a state in 2014. The crop record lists 33 Telangana districts, and all of them trace back to ten districts that the 2011 census filed under Andhra Pradesh. Adilabad and Mahabubnagar have enough years under the Telangana name to enter the 606. Seven more of the ten, among them Karimnagar and Warangal, enter through the years up to 2012–13 that the crop record files under Andhra Pradesh.

A renamed district such as Kalaburagi, called Gulbarga in 2011, keeps its old record. A district carved out after 2011 takes on its parent's village records, which describe the whole 2011 district, so two districts from one parent share the same facility and land figures. Districts that share a parent are grouped when uncertainty is measured. Daman and Diu find no match, and neither has meaningful farmland.

04 · The sample

Cities, most of Tripura and districts created in the last decade drop out of the estimates.

Coverage is villages in the survey over 2011 census villages in the same district. Hover or tap a removed slice to list its districts.

Nine matched districts have no Mission Antyodaya record at all. They are cities and small union territories: Puducherry's four districts, Chandigarh, Chennai, Hyderabad, Mumbai Suburban, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli. In another 16, the survey reaches fewer than a quarter of villages. Tripura accounts for eight of these, and its four 2011 districts sit between 5 and 16 percent coverage. Jammu and Kashmir has three and Manipur two. These districts are left out, because figures built from a small fraction of a district's villages cannot be compared with figures built from all of them.

The last rule asks for at least ten years of crop data. Most of the districts that fall below it were created after 2011, and thirty are in Telangana, where every district apart from Adilabad and Mahabubnagar has seven years or fewer under the Telangana name.

05 · Measuring diversity

The average district grows 21.4 crops in a year and has an effective number of crops of 4.9.

Effective number of crops = exp(−Σ p ln p), where p is a crop's share of the district's cropped area in a year. The number of main crops is 1 / Σ p². Evenness = effective number / count. Bins are half a crop wide.

Every measure is built from the share of a district's cropped land under each crop in a year, and a district's value is its average over the years from 1998–99 to 2019–20 that it reports. The average district in the sample grows 21.4 crops in a year. Most of its land sits under two or three of them, though, which gives it an effective number of crops of 4.9 and 3.5 main crops. Evenness averages 0.250.

Since all three are measured in crops and each gives rare crops less weight than the one before, they always fall in the order count, effective number, number of main crops. The effective number of crops is the main outcome throughout.

06 · One year at a time

Counting crops within each year halves the link between a district's count and the number of years it reports.

Districts are spread a little sideways so they do not sit on top of each other. Pooled counts every crop that appeared in any year. Year-by-year counts crops within each year and averages across years. The line is the median count of crops at each number of years.

A district's count of crops can be built two ways. Pooling the land over all its years counts every crop that ever appears, so a district observed for 22 years collects crops it grew once and looks richer than an identical district observed for eight. The pooled count averages 30.4 and correlates at 0.51 with the number of years observed. Counted within each year and then averaged, the count averages 20.6, and its correlation with years observed falls to 0.27.

Crop areas summed across seasons come to 194.9 million hectares in 2019–20, which is 92 percent of the 211.4 million hectares of gross sown area the Ministry of Agriculture reports for that year. Adding the seasons together therefore does not count a field twice.

07 · Checks against other sources

The village measures rank districts in close agreement with other sources.

correlation of levels, r rank correlation, rho

r compares levels and rho compares rankings, and 1 is perfect agreement. Cropping intensity is gross cropped area over net sown area, so 1.8 means the average field carries 1.8 crops a year. SECC is the Socio-Economic and Caste Census. Hover or tap a row for its means and district count.

Irrigated share from the 2019–20 village survey and the same share from the 2011 census village records rank 582 districts at a rank correlation of 0.812. Both are counts of the same land eight years apart, collected by different agencies. The survey's average is higher, 0.55 against 0.47, which fits panchayats reporting on their own villages. Scheduled Caste shares from the 2011 census and the 2012 SECC correlate at 0.942 across 609 districts, and Scheduled Tribe shares at 0.990. Summed to the state, the survey's irrigated share ranks 21 states almost exactly as the official land-use statistics for 2017–18 to 2019–20 do (rank correlation 0.91), even though it reads well above them in Jharkhand and Assam. Maharashtra and West Bengal publish no district net irrigated area for those years and cannot be checked.

Summing village records to the district reproduces SHRUG's own district file. Irrigated share, cropping intensity and mean holding size each correlate with SHRUG's ready-made district figures at 0.99 or more. Cropping intensity averages 1.75 across the 606 districts.

08 · What is estimated

Every comparison sets a district only against other districts in the same state.

Uncertainty ranges allow some districts to be more scattered than others and group districts from the same 2011 district. The 606 form 605 groups. Night lights 2023, Economic Census 2013. Hover or tap a row or a column heading for detail.

Each result is a regression of a crop diversity measure on one district measure, with the square of that measure added where the relationship curves. Every regression is a comparison within state (state fixed effects), so a district in Punjab is set only against other districts in Punjab. Anything that varies only between states, such as state agricultural policy, drops out and cannot be estimated.

Differences between states on their own account for 49 percent of the variation in the effective number of crops between districts, and irrigated share with its square raises that to 53 percent. Without the within-state comparison the same irrigation terms account for only 6.7 percent of the variation.

Irrigation, mandis, food storage warehouses and fertiliser shops were put where terrain, groundwater and existing farming already favoured them. Since the results compare districts at one point in time, they cannot show what adding a mandi to a district would do. Average farm size, cultivable area divided by the number of farmers, stands in for a farm census measure. Once irrigation is held constant within state its coefficient on the effective number of crops is +0.024, which is not statistically significant and may only mean the proxy is weak.

How this was estimated

Crop data. Areas are summed across seasons for each crop, district and year. Crops filed as whole-year almost never also appear under a named season, so summing seasons does not count land twice. The 2020–21 year is left out because only 13 districts report it. Each index is computed within a year, and a district's value is its average across the years it has between 1998–99 and 2019–20, 22 at most. The sample for estimates is 606 districts in 30 states, from 724 in the crop record.

1997–98. The first year of the record is left out of every average because its returns are broken. West Bengal files 50.8 million hectares of rabi niger seed, 28 percent of all area recorded in India that year, Odisha files 0.91 million hectares in all against 5.23 million the next year, and twelve states file crop lists under three fifths of their usual length.

Census shares count the whole population and SECC shares count rural households, which is why their levels differ. Against SHRUG's district file only ratios are compared, because the two cover slightly different sets of villages.

The comparisons. Every comparison is within state. The irrigation curve regresses the effective number of crops on irrigated share and its square. The irrigation source comparison adds the canal and surface-water shares of villages, with groundwater left out as the comparison, and holds irrigated share and its square, log mean holding size and the Scheduled Tribe share constant. The single-measure results are shown first on their own and then holding irrigated share, its square and log mean holding size constant. The facility comparisons hold constant irrigated share and its square, log mean holding size, the Scheduled Tribe share, log night lights, non-farm establishments per 1,000 people and the connectivity average, and apply a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the ten crop categories. Standard errors allow for unequal scatter and group districts by their 2011 district.
