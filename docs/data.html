Data and methods

Sources, the diversity measure and the district crosswalk

Crop area for fifty-four crops over twenty-three years, the effective number of crops as the measure of diversity, and the village-level record of irrigation, markets and landholding that describes each district.

Sources

Two datasets, joined at the district

Cropping comes from district-level area, production and yield statistics covering fifty-four crops across kharif, rabi and whole-year seasons, from 1997–98 to 2019–20. The series is compiled by the ISB India Data Portal from Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare publications. The fifty-four crops group as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fibre crops, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugar, fodder, and drugs and narcotics. It is reported crop area, so land outside the reporting frame, kitchen gardens among it, never appears.

Everything used to describe a district comes from SHRUG 2.1, an open dataset that puts Indian administrative records on a single village identifier so sources collected years apart can be joined village by village. Five bodies of village-level data are used. Each village record is summed to the district that contains it, so a district value is the total or the share across its villages.

Mission Antyodaya carries most of what a district is described by. It is a rural survey in which each gram panchayat reports what its villages have, from irrigated land to whether there is a mandi or a cold store. The answers are given by the panchayat and not measured, so levels can run high.

A village share is the fraction of a district's villages that report something, so a mandi village share of 0.030, which is the average across districts, means three villages in a hundred have a mandi. Cold storage averages 0.084 and a farmer producer organisation 0.231. A village share counts villages and does not weight them by population or area, so a large village and a small one count the same.

Cropping is averaged over 1997–98 to 2019–20, the census variables are 2011 and the facility variables are 2019–20, so the two sides of every relationship are measured in different decades.

Diversity is measured at district level from the agricultural statistics, and the village data supplies the characteristics each district is described by, because SHRUG holds no crop areas. That gives one row per district: 606 districts across 30 states, each carrying its average cropping pattern over 1997–98 to 2019–20 alongside its village characteristics.

The measure

Effective numbers of crops

A district's cropping pattern is a set of crops and the share of land each takes. An area share is a crop's share of the district's cropped land in a year, so a district with 70 per cent of its land under cereals has a cereal area share of 0.70, and the shares across crops sum to one.

Two things describe that pattern: how many crops appear, and how evenly land divides between them. Each is expressed as an effective number of crops, meaning the number of equally common crops that would give the diversity observed. The unit is crops, so the number reads the same in any district and in any year.

The average district grows 21.2 different crops in a year. Most of its land sits under two or three of them, so the effective number is 4.9. Read that as a district about as diverse as one growing five crops on equal land. The gap between 21.2 and 4.9 is what a plain count hides.

The three indices are one family and differ only in how much weight a rare crop gets. The subscript is that weight.

p is a crop's share of the district's cropped area in that year, and the shares across crops sum to one. Averages are across the 606 districts analysed.

D1 is the primary outcome throughout. Because the unit is crops, a district with D1 of 6 is twice as diverse as one with D1 of 3. An entropy in logarithms and a zero-to-one composite index do not have that property. The ordering D0 ≥ D1 ≥ D2 holds by construction, since dropping weight on rare crops can only lower the count.

Each index is computed within a year, and a district's value is the average across the years it has. Coverage is uneven: roughly a quarter of districts are observed for fewer than twenty of the twenty-three years, mostly because districts were being created throughout. Pooling land over the whole period would let a district accumulate crops it grew once, so a long-observed district would look more diverse than an identical short-observed one purely as a function of how long it had been watched. Across the full cropping series, pooling puts the average district at 29.8 crops and computing each year then averaging puts it at 20.1. The correlation between a district's diversity and the number of years it happens to be observed falls from 0.55 to 0.31 when the measure is built year by year.

Summing crop areas across seasons implies a national gross cropped area of 195 million hectares in 2019–20, against a published figure of 195 to 200 million, so seasonal rows are counting land once.

What is estimated

The effective number of crops is regressed on the district characteristic in question and, where the relationship bends, its square, with a separate intercept for each state and standard errors clustered on the pre-2011 parent district. A state fixed effect means each state gets its own baseline, so Punjab is compared with Punjab and Kerala with Kerala, and nothing in the result comes from Punjab and Kerala simply being different places. It costs something: anything that varies only between states, state agricultural policy among it, is absorbed and cannot be estimated.

Irrigated share and its square account for 6.5 per cent of the variation in the effective number of crops without state fixed effects and 53.0 per cent with them, and the turning point moves from an irrigated share of 0.513 to 0.282.

Geography

Reconciling districts across boundary change

SHRUG is built on 2011 census district boundaries and the cropping series uses present-day district names. Between them lie a decade of reorganisations, including the creation of Telangana, whose districts have no counterpart in the 2011 frame and have to be traced back to the parents they were carved from.

Districts are matched in order. An exact name match settles 534. A parent map carries 141 districts created after 2011 back to the district they were carved from. Name similarity within a state settles a further 47, of which 20 were not close enough to accept on the name alone and were checked by eye.

Where several present-day districts descend from a single pre-2011 parent, they necessarily carry identical village-level characteristics. Every one of them inherits the same village records, so on the explanatory side they are copies of each other and not independent observations. 64 of the 606 districts analysed, about one in ten, sit on a shared parent.

Clustering standard errors on the 2011 parent tells the estimator to count each parent once, which widens the confidence intervals to what the real number of independent districts supports. Each headline result is also re-estimated twice more: once weighting each district by one over the number of districts sharing its parent, and once on the 542 districts that have a parent to themselves.

Three district counts appear in this work. The cropping series has 725 districts. SHRUG's own frame has 631, fewer because it is the 2011 map. 722 of the 725 match a SHRUG district, and 606 survive two exclusions: districts where Mission Antyodaya reaches under a quarter of villages, and districts with fewer than ten years of cropping data. Every result quoted rests on those 606.

Cross-source comparison

How the village measures compare with independent sources

Irrigated share built from 2019 village records agrees with an independent measure built from the 2011 census village directory at a rank correlation of 0.812 across the 582 districts carrying both. Scheduled caste share from the 2011 census agrees with the same quantity from the Socio-Economic and Caste Census at r = 0.942 across 609 districts. Irrigation share, cropping intensity and mean holding size computed here agree with SHRUG's own district figures at correlations above 0.99.

Against published state land use statistics the two anchors differ. Irrigated share ranks states at rho = 0.852 with a mean absolute difference of 0.087. Cropping intensity ranks them at rho = 0.653 with a mean absolute difference of 0.273, which is why it is used to order districts and not to state a level.

rho compares the order in which two sources rank districts, where 1 is perfect agreement and 0 is none. r is the correlation between the levels themselves. A warning row means the measure ranks districts reliably and its level should not be quoted. Cropping intensity is gross cropped area over net sown area, so 1.8 means the average field is cropped 1.8 times a year. SECC is the Socio-Economic and Caste Census.

In 26 districts Mission Antyodaya reaches under a quarter of villages, most of them in Tripura, Jammu and Kashmir, Manipur and the island territories. Tripura's four districts sit between 0.05 and 0.16. Those districts are excluded from the results.

In Maharashtra, Jharkhand and Assam the survey puts irrigated share at 0.54, 0.45 and 0.35 against published state figures of 0.21, 0.12 and 0.11, a gap of 24 to 33 points, which is what a panchayat reporting on itself produces. Dropping those three states leaves the irrigation results unchanged. Their levels should not be quoted.

Limits

How to read these associations

Irrigation systems, market yards and cold stores are put where terrain, groundwater and existing agriculture already favoured them. Every association reported here describes how the cross-section is arranged. A district that installed a mandi tomorrow would not travel along any of these curves.

The rural infrastructure data is a single 2019–20 cross-section matched against cropping averaged over twenty-three years, which suits infrastructure that moves slowly. It supports no statement about change over time. The comparison is between districts, never within one district across years.

Mean holding size is cultivable area divided by the number of farmers, standing in for what an agricultural census would measure. It comes out null on every outcome, at +0.019 effective crops once irrigation, holding size and state baselines are in, which may be a comment on the proxy as much as on the relationship.

Procurement volumes, which would be the natural companion to the market results, are not available at district level from any open source and are absent here.
