Underneath

Data, and what it can carry

Sources, the diversity measure, how districts were reconciled across a decade of boundary changes, and the limits of a cross-section.

Sources

Two datasets, joined at the district

Cropping comes from district-level area, production and yield statistics covering fifty-four crops across kharif, rabi and whole-year seasons, from 1997–98 to 2019–20. The series is compiled by the ISB India Data Portal from Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare publications.

Everything on the explanatory side comes from SHRUG 2.1, an open geographic platform that harmonises Indian administrative datasets onto a stable village identifier. Four of its modules are used, all measured at village level and aggregated upward.

SHRUG carries no crop-area information of any kind, which fixes the design. Diversity has to be measured at district level from the agricultural statistics, and the village data supplies the characteristics a district is described by. Analysis is therefore a district cross-section of roughly six hundred districts across thirty states.

The measure

Effective numbers of crops

A district's cropping pattern is a set of crops and the share of land each takes. Two features of that pattern matter and they are not the same feature: how many crops appear, and how evenly the land divides between them. Rather than compress both into one score, each is expressed as the number of equally-common crops that would produce the diversity observed. That quantity is directly interpretable, needs no rescaling against the sample, and is comparable across places and years.

The three orders, and the ratio between the first two.
IndexDefinitionHow much a rare crop counts
D0crop richness, a plain countfully: a crop on one hectare counts as much as the staple
D1exp(Shannon)in proportion to its share of land
D21 / Σp²barely: governed by the dominant crops
evennessD1 / D0balance alone, with the count divided out

D1 is the primary outcome throughout. Because the unit is crops, a doubling of D1 means diversity genuinely doubled, which is not true of an entropy in logarithms and certainly not of an index rescaled to run between zero and one. The ordering D0 ≥ D1 ≥ D2 holds by construction, which makes the family self-checking.

Each index is computed within a year and then averaged across the years a district has, rather than computed once on land pooled across the whole period. That matters because coverage is uneven: roughly a quarter of districts are observed for fewer than twenty of the twenty-three years, mostly because districts were being created throughout. Pooling would let a district accumulate crops it grew once, so a long-observed district would look more diverse than an identical short-observed one purely as a function of how long it had been watched.

Reconstructed national gross cropped area from these files lands in the range published figures put it, which is the check that summing across seasons counts land once rather than twice.

Geography

Reconciling districts across boundary change

The two datasets sit on different maps. SHRUG is built on 2011 census boundaries; the cropping series uses present-day district names. Between them lie a decade of reorganisations, including the creation of Telangana, whose districts do not exist in the census frame at all and have to be traced back to the parents they were carved from.

Matching proceeds in three steps: exact name match, then an explicit parent map for districts created after 2011, then conservative fuzzy matching within state with every accepted match reviewed by eye. Manual mappings are asserted against the census district list before use, so a mistyped target fails loudly rather than silently dropping a district.

How districts were matched

Method used for each district in the cropping series.

Three districts go unmatched: Mumbai and the two island territories of Daman and Diu, none of which carries meaningful agriculture.

One consequence needs stating plainly. Where several present-day districts descend from a single pre-2011 parent, they necessarily carry identical village-level characteristics. Around a fifth of the analysis sample is in that position. Every model therefore clusters its standard errors on the parent district, and each headline result is re-estimated both with inverse weighting by the number of siblings and on a sample that drops shared parents entirely.

Checks

What the covariates were tested against

Village aggregates were validated before use. Irrigation shares built from 2019 village records were compared against an entirely independent measure built from the 2011 census village directory, and against published state irrigation statistics. Caste shares from the census were compared against the same quantity from the socio-economic census. District aggregates were reproduced against the platform's own district files.

Two coverage limits carry through into the results. Mission Antyodaya covers under a quarter of villages in a small number of districts, concentrated in Tripura, Kerala and parts of the northeast; those districts are flagged and excluded rather than averaged in. And in Maharashtra, Jharkhand and Assam the survey reports irrigation levels well above published figures, which is a property of self-reported panchayat data. Dropping those three states leaves the irrigation results unchanged, but their levels should not be quoted.

Limits

What this design cannot do

The central limitation is not statistical. Irrigation systems, market yards, cold stores and fertiliser dealerships are placed where terrain, aquifers, politics and existing agriculture already favoured them. Nothing here is randomly assigned and nothing here is an experiment. Every association reported is a description of how the cross-section is arranged, and a district that installed a mandi tomorrow would not travel along any of these curves.

Three narrower limits are worth naming. The rural infrastructure data is a single 2019–20 cross-section matched against cropping averaged over two decades, which is defensible for slow-moving infrastructure and not for anything that should be called a change. Mean holding size is inferred from cultivable area over the number of farmers rather than measured from an agricultural census, and it is null on every outcome, which is at least as likely to be a comment on the proxy as on the relationship. And procurement volumes, which would be the natural companion to the market results, are not available at district level from any open source and are absent here.

The whole pipeline, from the raw agricultural statistics through to every figure on this site, runs end to end from a single command and produces the numbers shown without manual transcription.
