Share of each district’s cropped area under food crops

Each district’s average over the years it reports, 1997–98 to 2019–20.

In five of every six districts on this map, food crops cover at least nine tenths of the cropped area. The districts with the lowest shares are mostly cotton districts in Telangana, Gujarat and Maharashtra.

How to read this map

Brighter cyan means a larger share of the cropped area under food crops, and dark slate means a smaller one. Unshaded districts have no agricultural record. Hover over a district to see its food share, its effective number of crops and its largest crops. A crop counts as food when it belongs to a food group, whatever later happens to the harvest. The effective number of crops is the number of equally sized crops that would give the same spread of land.

Food crops are cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruit, vegetables, sugar and spices. The remaining land is under fibre crops, fodder crops, and drugs and narcotics.

What counts as food

A crop counts as food if its category does. Cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruit, vegetables, sugar and spices count. Fibre crops, fodder, and drugs and narcotics do not; they make up the rest of the land.

The split goes by crop. Maize counts as food here even when it is grown for poultry feed.

Where the land is not growing food

The food share is lowest in the cotton belt across Gujarat, Maharashtra and Telangana, and in the guar districts of western Rajasthan. Of the 63 districts with less than four fifths of their land under food crops, 41 have an effective number of crops above the median district. Cotton shares their land with several other crops.
