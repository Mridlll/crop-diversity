Overview

Crop diversity across Indian districts

Most districts record twenty or so crops in a year and give most of their land to two or three of them.

01 · What is measured

The average district records about 20 crops a year, yet spreads its land as if it grew about five.

Each square is 1 percent of the district's cropped area, averaged over the years it reports. Land cropped in two seasons counts twice. Hover or tap a square for its crop.

The average district records 20.6 crops in a year. That count of crops gives a crop on half the district's land no more weight than one on 0.1 percent of it.

The effective number of crops weights each crop by its share of gross cropped area, the land under crops in a year, with a field counted once for every season it is sown. It is the number of equally sized crops that would spread the land the way the district does. The average district's effective number is 4.7, about a quarter of its count.

Sipahijala in Tripura records 20 crops in an average year, yet rice covers 93 percent of its cropped land and leaves it an effective number of 1.5. Chamarajanagara in Karnataka records 33 crops, and because its largest, maize, takes only 15 percent of the land, its effective number of 14.3 is the highest of any district.

02 · Three measures

In nearly every district the effective number of crops sits far below the count of crops.

Each dot is a district averaged over the years it reports. Both axes are numbers of crops on the same scale, so the vertical distance below the diagonal is also a number of crops. Hover or tap a dot for its district.

Both measures belong to one family, the Hill numbers, which differ only in how much weight small crops get. A third member, the number of main crops, discounts small crops further still, so it mostly reflects a district's two or three largest; its national mean is 3.3. In every district the count is at least the effective number, which in turn is at least the number of main crops. The Measurement section works through all three.

Evenness divides the effective number by the count and reaches one only when every crop a district records takes the same area. Its national mean is 0.26, and seven districts in ten sit below 0.3. Only a handful of places that record a single crop, such as Mumbai Suburban and Diu, reach one.

03 · Where

The effective number of crops is highest on the southern plateau, in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.

District means over the years each district reports. The top and bottom 2 percent of districts take the end colours of the scale. White lines are state borders. Hatched districts have no crop area in the record, mostly the Delhi districts, Kolkata, and districts created after the record ends. Hover or tap a district for its values.

The eight districts with the highest effective number of crops are all in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. Chamarajanagara leads at 14.3, followed by Salem at 13.0 and Dharmapuri at 12.7. In each of the eight the largest crop takes between 14 and 22 percent of the land.

Most districts in Punjab, Odisha and Tripura have an effective number below three, as do more than half of those in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal and Assam. Many of the eastern districts keep long crop lists all the same. Raipur records 33 crops even though rice covers 83 percent of its land, and across India 48 districts record 20 or more crops while their effective number stays below three.

04 · States

The average district in Chhattisgarh records 31.9 crops in a year, more than in any other state, yet its effective number is only 3.2.

count of crops effective number of crops

District means within each state, for the 29 states with at least four districts. Hover or tap a row for its evenness and number of districts.

Karnataka's districts spread their land the widest, with an average effective number of 8.1, and Andhra Pradesh comes next at 6.9. At the other end, Punjab, Puducherry, Odisha and Tripura all average below three.

Chhattisgarh and Punjab reach a low effective number from opposite directions. Although Chhattisgarh records more crops than any other state, rice takes so much of its land that its evenness, 0.10, is the lowest of any state. Punjab records the fewest crops, 9.0, but spreads its land across them more evenly than the national average; in Ludhiana wheat and rice each take close to half.

05 · The largest crop

Rice or wheat takes the most land in about seven of every ten districts.

Each dot is a district, placed in the row of the crop with the largest share of its cropped area. The percentage is that crop's mean share of the land in the districts where it leads. A crop's area share is its area divided by the district's total cropped area, adding all seasons together, averaged over the years each district reports.

Rice is the largest single crop in 351 districts, and where it leads it takes 60 percent of the cropped land on average. Wheat leads in 147 districts, with 42 percent of the land. Together they lead in 498 of the 724 districts, while no other crop leads in more than 35, the count for maize.

Cotton leads in 33 districts and soybean in 28. Coconut leads in 19, 17 of them in Kerala and Tamil Nadu and two in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, and there it takes 65 percent of the land on average. Because land cropped in two seasons counts twice, a district that grows rice in the monsoon and wheat in winter on the same fields shows both crops at full area.

In 166 of the 724 districts the largest crop holds at least two thirds of the land in an average year, and rice is that crop in 146 of them. Assam and Odisha have 27 such districts each, followed by Chhattisgarh with 20, Jharkhand with 15 and West Bengal with 12. The measurement page sets these near-monoculture districts beside districts that grow few crops and districts that split the year between rice and wheat.

06 · Land use

In the top quartile of districts by effective number of crops, cereals take half the land, and pulses and oilseeds a third between them.

Mean share of cropped area in the average district of each quartile, with districts ranked on the effective number of crops. Spices, sugar, vegetables, fruit, fodder and tobacco are pooled as other.

In the bottom quartile, cereals take 77 percent of the cropped land of the average district, against 49 percent in the top quartile. Across the four quartiles pulses rise from 5 percent of the land to 17, oilseeds from 10 percent to 17, and fibre crops, mostly cotton, from 2 percent to 7.7.

Fruit stays between 0.3 and 1.2 percent of the land in every quartile and vegetables between 0.8 and 2.3. Since the crop statistics record horticulture less completely than field crops, these fruit and vegetable shares are a floor.

07 · Over time

The effective number of crops stayed between 5.2 and 5.9 in every year from 1998–99 to 2019–20.

count of crops crops on at least 0.1% of the land effective number of crops

Means weighted by cropped area, over districts that report in every year. 2019 marks the 2019–20 agricultural year. Hover or tap for a year's values.

The effective number of crops was 5.6 in 1998–99 and 5.5 in 2019–20, and in no year between did it leave the band from 5.2 to 5.9. Over two decades the spread of India's cropped land across crops hardly changed.

The count of crops rose from 21.2 to 25.3 over the same years, yet all of that rise sits in crops holding less than 0.1 percent of a district's land. Counting only crops above that line, the average district grew 14.4 crops at both ends of the period. Most of the rise traces to states that filed short crop lists in 1998–99, as the Measurement section shows.

08 · District by district

Between the early 2000s and the late 2010s the median district's effective number of crops fell by less than a fifth of a crop.

Each district's mean over 1998–99 to 2004–05 against its mean over 2013–14 to 2019–20. Cyan dots rose and grey dots fell. Hover or tap a dot for its district.

Between 1998–2004 and 2013–2019 the effective number of crops fell in 297 districts and rose in 203, and the median district lost 0.17 of a crop from a typical value near five. Weighted by cropped land the fall is 0.20, which is not statistically significant once each state's swings in reporting are allowed for.

The count of crops moved the other way in the median district, up by 0.29, with 258 districts rising and 238 falling, although that change is not statistically significant either. Evenness fell in 287 districts and rose in 213, with a median fall of 0.008.

09 · Further

In a comparison within state (state fixed effects), heavily irrigated districts spread their land across fewer crops.

Estimates Irrigation

Within state, the effective number of crops changes little until irrigation covers about half of sown area and falls once it covers more, and the fall happens inside each season. Districts already heavily irrigated in 1991 grow fewer crops today.

 Estimates Markets and inputs

Where more rural people live near a weekly haat, districts record more crops without spreading their land more evenly. One standard deviation, 15 percentage points of the rural population, goes with about 0.9 more crops on the count but no clear change in the effective number.

How this was estimated

Data. District crop area and production come from the ISB India Data Portal, for 54 crops and the agricultural years 1998–99 to 2019–20. A crop's share is its area divided by the district's total cropped area in that year, adding all seasons, so land cropped twice counts twice. Every measure is computed within each year and then averaged over the years a district reports.

Measures. With pi the share of crop i, the count of crops (D0) is the number of crops with positive area. The effective number of crops (D1) is exp(−Σ pi ln pi), the exponential of the Shannon index. The number of main crops (D2) is 1 / Σ pi2, the inverse Simpson index. Evenness is D1 / D0. D0 ≥ D1 ≥ D2 holds in every district.

Every descriptive figure uses all 724 districts, and the irrigation and market estimates use the 606-district estimation sample. Across the 724 the median count of crops is 21.7 and the median effective number of crops is 4.2.

Three sets of districts. The 724 districts with a usable crop record are used for every descriptive figure. Of these, 606 also match the SHRUG 2.1 village data and have enough villages covered in the 2019–20 village survey, and every estimate uses them. 500 report in all 22 years and are used for the national series and the district change. The full set grows from 543 districts reporting in 1998–99 to 699 in 2019–20, so a series on all districts would mix change within districts with districts entering the record. On all districts the effective number of crops runs from 5.6 in 1998–99 to 5.4 in 2019–20.

First year. Every district average and every change over time starts in 1998–99 because the 1997–98 returns are broken. West Bengal files 50.8 million hectares of rabi niger seed, 28 percent of all area recorded in India that year. Odisha files 0.91 million hectares in all against 5.23 million the next year, Chhattisgarh's kharif area is 0.48 million hectares against 4.56 million, and twelve states file crop lists under three fifths of their usual length.

Two weightings. The national series weights districts by cropped area and compares single years, 1998–99 with 2019–20. The district change counts each district once and compares seven-year means. Uncertainty on either change groups districts by state, since states file the returns. On that basis neither the effective number of crops nor the count of crops has a statistically significant trend.

Quartiles. The shares in figure 06 are means of district shares within each quartile of the 724 districts ranked on the effective number of crops, so each district counts once whatever its area.

Horticulture. The crop statistics record horticulture less completely than field crops, so fruit and vegetable shares are a floor.

Map. District boundaries follow the current map. Where a current district carries a different name in the crop record, such as Prayagraj for Allahabad or Y.S.R. for Kadapa, the two are joined by name.

Estimates. Every irrigation and market estimate is a comparison within state. 162 districts in the crop record share their 2011 district with at least one other district, 64 of them in the estimation sample. Districts that share a 2011 district are grouped when uncertainty is measured. Inside the estimation sample only Amethi and Sultanpur share one, so the 606 districts form 605 groups. Without those 64 the sample is 542 districts. Market and input facilities are measured as the share of a district's rural population living in a village that has one, using Census 2011 village populations, so a large village counts for more than a small one.
