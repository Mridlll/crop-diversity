Crop diversity across Indian districts

How many crops each of 725 Indian districts grows in a year, and how evenly it spreads its cropped land across them, from 1997–98 to 2019–20.

01 · What is measured

The average district records about 20 crops in a year and spreads its land as if it grew about five.

Each square is 1 percent of the district's cropped area, averaged over the years it reports. Land cropped in two seasons counts twice. Hover or tap a square for its crop. District crop area and production statistics, 1997–98 to 2019–20.

Each year districts report the area under each of up to 54 crops. Across 725 districts, the average district records 20.4 crops in a year. A plain count gives a crop on half the district's land the same weight as a crop on a tenth of a percent of it.

The effective number of crops weights each crop by its share of the cropped land. It is the number of crops that, each given an equal share of the land, would spread it the way the district actually does. The average district has an effective number of 4.7 crops, about a quarter of its count of crops.

Sipahijala in Tripura records 20 crops in an average year and puts 93 percent of its cropped land under rice, which gives it an effective number of 1.5 crops. Chamarajanagara in Karnataka records 33 crops, and its largest, maize, takes 15 percent of the land. Its effective number of 14.3 crops is the highest of any district.

02 · Three measures

In nearly every district the effective number of crops sits far below the count of crops.

Each dot is a district averaged over the years it reports. Both axes are numbers of crops on the same scale, so the vertical distance below the diagonal is also a number of crops. The five dots on the diagonal record a single crop. Hover or tap a dot for its district. 725 districts.

The count of crops and the effective number of crops both come from one family of measures, the Hill numbers, which differ in how much weight they give to crops on small areas. The count gives every crop the same weight. The effective number of crops is the exponential of the Shannon index of land shares. A third member, the number of main crops, squares each crop's share before adding them up, so a crop on 1 percent of the land counts for a hundredth of a crop on 10 percent. Its national mean is 3.3, and it mostly reflects a district's two or three largest crops. In every district it is lower than or equal to the effective number of crops, which is lower than or equal to the count.

Evenness is the effective number of crops divided by the count of crops. It equals one when every crop a district records takes the same area. The national mean is 0.26, and 68 percent of districts fall below 0.3. Mumbai, Mumbai Suburban, Daman, Diu and South Salmara Mancachar sit on the diagonal with an evenness of one because each records a single crop.

03 · Where

The effective number of crops is highest on the southern plateau, in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.

District means over the years each district reports. The top and bottom 2 percent of districts take the end colours of the scale. White lines are state borders. Hatched districts have no crop area in the record, mostly the Delhi districts, Kolkata, and districts created after the record ends. Hover or tap a district for its values. 725 districts.

The eight districts with the highest effective number of crops are all in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. Chamarajanagara leads at 14.3, followed by Salem at 13.0 and Dharmapuri at 12.7. In each of the eight the largest crop takes between 14 and 22 percent of the land.

Most districts in Punjab, Odisha and Tripura have an effective number below three, and so do more than half of those in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal and Assam. Many of the eastern districts record long crop lists. Raipur records 32 crops and puts 82 percent of its land under rice. Across India, 46 districts record 20 or more crops and have an effective number of crops below three.

04 · States

The average district in Chhattisgarh records 31.6 crops in a year, more than in any other state, and has an effective number of 3.2 crops.

Karnataka has the highest state mean, with an effective number of 8.0 crops and a count of 29.7, and Andhra Pradesh follows at 6.9. Nagaland and Uttarakhand come next, at 6.4 and 6.2. The lowest four are Punjab at 2.8, Odisha at 2.5, Puducherry at 2.4 and Tripura at 2.0.

Chhattisgarh's evenness of 0.11 is the lowest of the 29 states, because rice takes most of its land however many crops are recorded beside it. Punjab has the lowest count of crops of any state, at 9.1. Its evenness of 0.32 is above the national mean of 0.26, so its low effective number comes from its short list of crops. Wheat and rice each take close to half the land in Ludhiana.

05 · The largest crop

Rice or wheat takes the most land in about seven of every ten districts.

Each dot is a district, placed in the row of the crop with the largest share of its cropped area. The percentage is that crop's mean share of the land in the districts where it leads. A crop's area share is its area divided by the district's total cropped area, adding all seasons together, averaged over the years each district reports. 725 districts.

Rice is the largest single crop in 350 districts, and where it leads it takes 59 percent of the cropped land on average. Wheat leads in 147 districts, with 42 percent of the land. Together they lead in 497 of the 725 districts. No other crop leads in more than 36, the count for maize.

Cotton leads in 33 districts and soybean in 28. Coconut leads in 19, 17 of them in Kerala and Tamil Nadu and two in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, and there it takes 65 percent of the land on average. Land cropped in two seasons counts twice, so a district that grows rice in the monsoon and wheat in winter on the same fields shows both crops at full area.

In 166 of the 725 districts the largest crop holds at least two thirds of the land in an average year, and rice is that crop in 145 of them. Assam and Odisha have 27 such districts each, followed by Chhattisgarh with 19, Jharkhand with 15 and West Bengal with 12. The measurement page sets these near-monoculture districts beside districts that grow few crops and districts that split the year between rice and wheat.

06 · Land use

In the quarter of districts with the highest effective number of crops, cereals take half the land, and pulses and oilseeds take a third between them.

Mean share of cropped area in the average district of each quarter. Districts are ranked on the effective number of crops and split into four groups of about 181. Spices, sugar, vegetables, fruit, fodder and tobacco are pooled as other. Select a category to read its share in every quarter. 725 districts.

We rank the 725 districts on their effective number of crops and split them into four groups of about 181. In the average district of the lowest quarter, cereals take 77 percent of the cropped land. In the highest quarter they take 50 percent.

Pulses rise from 5 percent of the land in the lowest quarter to 17 percent in the highest, and oilseeds from 10 percent to 17. Fibre crops, mostly cotton, rise from 2 percent to 7.5. Fruit stays between 0.3 and 1.2 percent of the land in every quarter and vegetables between 0.8 and 2.2. The crop statistics record field crops in detail and horticulture less completely, so these fruit and vegetable shares are a floor.

07 · Over time

The effective number of crops stayed between 5.2 and 5.9 in every year from 1998–99 to 2019–20.

count of crops effective number of crops

Area-weighted means across the 429 districts that report in all 23 years, so no district enters or leaves the series. 2019 marks the 2019–20 agricultural year. Hover or tap for a year's values.

The series weights each district by its cropped area, so a large wheat district counts for more than a small hill district. It uses only the 429 districts that report in every year. The effective number of crops was 5.5 in 1998–99 and 5.4 in 2019–20, and it stayed between 5.2 and 5.9 in every year in between. The first year of the record, 1997–98, is lower on every measure, with an effective number of 3.8.

Over the same years the count of crops rose from 21.2 to 24.9. With the effective number flat, the crops added to district lists took little land.

08 · District by district

Of the 429 districts that report every year, 264 ended the period with a lower effective number of crops.

Each district's mean over 1998–99 to 2004–05 against its mean over 2013–14 to 2019–20. Cyan dots rose and grey dots fell. Districts unweighted. Hover or tap a dot for its district. 429 districts reporting in all 23 years.

We compare each district's average over the seven years from 1998–99 to 2004–05 with its average over the seven years to 2019–20. The median district's effective number of crops fell by 0.19, against a typical level near five. The effective number rose in the other 165. Evenness fell in 229 districts and rose in 200, with a median fall of 0.004.

This comparison counts every district once, however much land it has. On it the median district's count of crops fell by 0.29 between the two periods, falling in 226 districts and rising in 199. The national series in the previous figure weights each district by its cropped area, and on that weighting the count of crops rose from 21.2 to 24.9.

09 · Further

Crop diversity is set against irrigation and against rural markets in two further analyses.

Estimates Irrigation

Compared within each state, the effective number of crops is highest in districts with about 28 percent of sown area irrigated and falls in more irrigated districts. Other versions of the comparison put the peak between 24 and 36 percent. Canals, surface water and groundwater are also compared one by one.

 Estimates Markets and inputs

Mandis, weekly haats, fertiliser shops and seven other facilities, each measured as the share of a district's villages that have one. A rise of 11.7 percent of villages with a weekly haat, one standard deviation, goes with about 0.9 more crops on the count of crops. On the effective number of crops the same rise goes with about 0.06 more, too small to tell from chance (p = 0.56).

A district with one or two years of record is drawn the same way as one with twenty-three. Use these maps for crop composition and food energy.

All the estimates use 606 districts that also appear in the 2019–20 village survey. Each comparison uses a state baseline, which compares each district only with districts in its own state. That removes everything a state's districts share, such as policy, rainfall and the way records are kept. Economists call it a state fixed effect. On the irrigation comparison the state baseline raises the share of variation across districts that the model accounts for from 6 percent to 53 percent, and moves the peak from 51 percent of sown area irrigated to 28 percent.

These are associations across districts at one point in time. Irrigation canals and market infrastructure were built where land, politics and existing farming already favoured them. The code and data are in the repository.

How this was estimated

Data. District crop area and production come from the ISB India Data Portal, for 54 crops and the agricultural years 1997–98 to 2019–20. A crop's share is its area divided by the district's total cropped area in that year, adding all seasons, so land cropped twice counts twice. Every measure is computed within each year and then averaged over the years a district reports.

Measures. With pi the share of crop i, the count of crops (D0) is the number of crops with positive area. The effective number of crops (D1) is exp(−Σ pi ln pi), the exponential of the Shannon index. The number of main crops (D2) is 1 / Σ pi2, the inverse Simpson index. Evenness is D1 / D0. D0 ≥ D1 ≥ D2 holds in every district.

Every figure above uses all 725 districts. The irrigation and market estimates use the 606-district estimation sample. Across the 725 the median count of crops is 21.6 and the median effective number of crops is 4.1.

Three sets of districts. The 725 districts with a usable crop record are used for every descriptive figure. Of these, 606 also match the SHRUG 2.1 village data and have enough villages covered in the 2019–20 village survey, and every estimate uses them. 429 report in all 23 years and are used for the national series and the district change. The full set grows from 484 districts reporting in 1997–98 to 699 in 2019–20, so a series on all districts would mix change within districts with districts entering the record. On all districts the effective number of crops runs from 5.6 in 1998–99 to 5.4 in 2019–20.

First year. 1997–98 is lower on every measure than any later year, with an effective number of crops of 3.8 against 5.2 to 5.9 afterwards, so the change window starts in 1998–99.

Two weightings. The national series weights districts by cropped area and compares single years, 1998–99 with 2019–20. The district change counts each district once and compares seven-year means. Over the same period the area-weighted count of crops rises from 21.2 to 24.9, and the median district's count falls by 0.29 between the two seven-year means.

Quarters of districts. The shares in figure 06 are means of district shares within each quarter of the 725 districts ranked on the effective number of crops, so each district counts once whatever its area.

Horticulture. The crop statistics record horticulture less completely than field crops, so fruit and vegetable shares are a floor.

Map. District boundaries follow the current map. Where a current district carries a different name in the crop record, such as Prayagraj for Allahabad or Y.S.R. for Kadapa, the two are joined by name.

Estimates. Every irrigation and market model includes the state baseline. 162 districts in the crop record share their 2011 district with at least one other district, 64 of them in the estimation sample. Districts that share a 2011 district are grouped when uncertainty is measured. Inside the estimation sample only Amethi and Sultanpur share one, so the 606 districts form 605 groups. Without those 64 the sample is 542 districts. Market and input variables are shares of villages with the facility, so every village counts the same regardless of its size. A district of many small villages and one large market town scores low even if most of its people live near a market.
